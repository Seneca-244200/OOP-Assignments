# Academic Integrity Violations at Seneca Polytechnic

Seneca Polytechnic defines an **academic integrity violation** as an attempt to gain an unfair advantage in a formal assessment, including coursework, assignments, projects, tests, and examinations.

The following eight categories are based on the definitions in Seneca Polytechnic's **Academic Integrity Policy**.

> **Important:** The examples below are provided to help students understand the policy. They are not intended to be an exhaustive list. If you are unsure whether something is permitted, ask your professor **before** submitting the work.

## 1. Cheating

**Cheating** means obtaining, attempting to obtain, or helping another person obtain academic credit or an improved result through dishonest or deceptive means.

Examples may include:

- copying another student's answers or work;
- giving answers or completed work to another student;
- unauthorized collaboration;
- using unauthorized resources during an assessment;
- taking individual credit for group work;
- helping another student commit an academic integrity violation;
- using undeclared or unauthorized AI tools.

## 2. Contract Cheating

**Contract cheating** occurs when a student submits academic work created by a third party as though it were their own.

The third party may be:

- another student;
- a friend or family member;
- a paid service;
- an online service or website;
- any other person or organization producing the work for the student.

Payment is **not required** for the behaviour to be considered contract cheating.

## 3. Plagiarism

**Plagiarism** means using another person's, group's, or organization's work and presenting it as your own without properly citing the source.

This may include using or modifying:

- words or written material;
- images;
- ideas;
- logic or problem-solving approaches;
- calculations;
- computer code;
- other academic or creative work.

Changing variable names, formatting, wording, or small portions of another person's work does not make that work your own.

## 4. Self-Plagiarism

**Self-plagiarism** is the unauthorized reuse of your own work from a previous assignment or assessment.

For example, submitting all or part of an assignment that you completed in a previous semester or another course may be a violation unless your professor has specifically permitted it.

## 5. Unauthorized Aid

**Unauthorized aid** means using supports or resources that have not been approved for completing coursework or an assessment.

Examples may include:

- unauthorized collaboration with another person;
- artificial intelligence tools;
- websites or Internet resources;
- smartphones, smartwatches, or other electronic devices;
- notes, reference material, or software not permitted by the professor.

What is considered an authorized aid may vary from one assessment to another. Always follow the instructions provided by your professor.

## 6. Undeclared AI Use

**Undeclared AI use** means using artificial intelligence to generate part or all of an assessment without providing the required disclosure explaining how and to what extent AI was used.

A disclosure does **not** automatically make AI use acceptable. If the professor has prohibited AI for an assessment, using AI may also be considered **unauthorized aid**.

Students must follow the AI-use instructions provided for each course and assessment.

## 7. Falsification / Fabrication

**Falsification or fabrication** means creating, altering, withholding, forging, or misrepresenting information in order to gain an academic advantage.

Examples may include:

- falsifying medical or academic documentation;
- altering records or assessment information;
- fabricating data, results, or observations;
- changing information and presenting it as original or authentic;
- misrepresenting another student's academic work.

## 8. Impersonation

**Impersonation** occurs when a person takes a test, examination, or other assessment on behalf of another student, either in person or online, with that student's knowledge and consent.

Arranging for another person to complete an assessment on your behalf is an academic integrity violation.

## Use of Cited Material in Homework and Projects

For **homework, assignments, and projects**, the professor may explicitly allow students to copy, reuse, adapt, or incorporate **limited portions of existing work** when the source is properly cited.

When this is permitted:

- the source must be clearly identified and cited;
- the copied or adapted portion must follow the professor's instructions;
- the student must still demonstrate their own work and understanding;
- copied or reused portions may receive **reduced marks or no marks**, depending on the assessment requirements and marking scheme.

This permission applies **only when the professor has explicitly allowed it**. Proper citation does not make otherwise unauthorized copying acceptable.

If you are unsure whether a source, code fragment, AI-generated material, example, or another person's work may be used, **ask your professor before using it**.
